Wiring the detector

One XIAO ESP32-S3, one passive buzzer, one 8-LED NeoPixel bar. The two signal pins below are fixed in hardware_manager.h — wire to match them.

Seeed XIAO ESP32-S3 · NeoPixel D1 / GPIO2 · Buzzer D2 / GPIO3

Schematic

Reading your board D# = GPIO#

  • Seeed silk-screens the pads D0–D10; the firmware names the same pads by GPIO number. D1 = GPIO2 (NeoPixel), D2 = GPIO3 (buzzer).
  • The pad marked VUSB on the underside is the 5V pad — USB bus voltage. Power pads (VUSB/GND/3V3) sit on the opposite edge from the D-pins.

Assembly

  1. Power the LED bar from 5V. XIAO 5V → bar VCC/5V, XIAO GND → bar GND. The 5V pad is USB bus power — what you run on.
  2. Data line. XIAO D1 (GPIO2) → bar DIN. Follow the arrows on the bar's silkscreen; wire the input end. Optional 330–470 Ω resistor sits in series right at DIN.
  3. Buzzer. XIAO D2 (GPIO3) → buzzer +, XIAO GND → buzzer −. A small passive buzzer (<30 mA) drives straight off the pin. 3-pin module? VCC→3V3, GND→GND, I/O→D2.
  4. Share the ground. Bar GND and buzzer − both land on the single XIAO GND pad. That shared return is the grey junction in the diagram.
  5. Verify. Re-flash with python flash.py --tier 1 --port COM3. On boot you get a jingle and an LED blink; a match then flashes the strip and plays that category's buzzer pattern.

Watch for

Power budget ≈480 mA

  • 8× WS2812 at full white peaks near 480 mA. SignalSweep only flashes short colored bursts, so USB 5V is fine.
  • Add a 1000 µF cap across the bar's 5V↔GND to absorb inrush when all eight snap on.

Gotchas clones

  • 3.3 V data into a 5 V-powered clone bar usually works; if LED 1 flickers, add the series resistor or power the bar from 3V3 instead.
  • Wrong end (wiring DOUT) lights nothing — flip to DIN.
  • Active buzzer = one fixed pitch: you'll hear each pattern's rhythm but not its tones. Passive is the intended part.
