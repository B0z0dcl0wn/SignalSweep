Wiring the C5 detector

All you need is the board. A bare XIAO ESP32-C5 with its dual-band U.FL antenna is a working detector: plug it into USB, connect the app, and it streams everything it hears. The buzzer, LED bar and DS3231 clock are optional add-ons, and the firmware checks at boot for what is fitted. The microSD slot is reserved: its wiring is drawn so a harness can include it, but the firmware does not use it yet.

Assembly

  1. One wire per pad — chain the rest. The XIAO has one GND, one 3V3 and one 5V, each a tiny through-hole, and with every part fitted there are four grounds. Two wires shoved into one hole is how a joint cracks. So each XIAO pad gets exactly one wire, and power and ground hop from part to part (see Power chains). Any doubling up happens on a part's bigger pad or header pin.
  2. LED bar. XIAO 5V → bar VCC, XIAO GND → one bar GND pad, XIAO D1 (GPIO0) → bar DIN. Wire the input end (the silkscreen arrows point into the LEDs). Optional 330–470 Ω resistor in series right at DIN.
  3. Buzzer. XIAO D2 (GPIO25) → buzzer +, buzzer − → the bar's other GND pad. A small passive buzzer (<30 mA) drives straight off the pin. 3-pin module? VCC → the 3V3 chain, GND → the ground chain, I/O → D2.
  4. DS3231 clock. On its 4-pin end: SDA ← D4 (GPIO23), SCL ← D5 (GPIO24), VCC ← XIAO 3V3, GND ← the bar's 2nd GND pad. The ZS-042 joins same-named pins across both headers, so its 6-pin end passes VCC and GND on. It already has pull-ups on SDA/SCL; add none.
  5. microSD (reserved — skip it for now). If you build it into the harness anyway: CS ← D3 (GPIO7), SCK ← D8 (GPIO8), MISO → D9 (GPIO9), MOSI ← D10 (GPIO10), VCC and GND from the RTC's 6-pin end (a VCC 5V module: from the bar's VCC pad instead). Pin order varies by module; match by label. The firmware does not drive it yet.
  6. Antenna. Swap the stock 2.4 GHz antenna for a dual-band U.FL one. The C5's whole point is 5 GHz; the one in the box throws it away.
  7. Verify. Flash from the web flasher, or python flash.py --board c5 --port COM5. On boot you get a short jingle and an LED blink (if fitted). The serial log says [RTC] DS3231 ok, clock set, [RTC] DS3231 lost power, waiting for a host, or [RTC] none fitted, waiting for a host. Connect the app: Settings › Device identity › Clock shows whether an RTC was found and that the phone synced it. Then trigger a match (an AirTag near it, or edit a signature): the bar flashes and the buzzer plays that category's pattern — ALPR / camera two long beeps, body cam long-short-short, drone a rising trill, tracker fast ticking. BOOT (GPIO28) is the onboard button: tap for a 2-minute advertising window, hold 5 s to factory-reset.

Watch for

Power budget ≈480 mA

  • 8× WS2812 at full white peaks near 480 mA. SignalSweep only flashes short colored bursts, so USB 5V is fine.
  • Add a 1000 µF cap across the bar's 5V↔GND to absorb inrush when all eight snap on.
  • The DS3231 and an SD module add a few mA idle, and roughly 100 mA peaks on SD writes. On a weak battery, budget for those.

Gotchas clones

  • ZS-042 + a CR2032: the board has a trickle-charge circuit (a resistor and diode near the header) meant for a rechargeable LIR2032. Fed from 3V3 — which is how this guide wires it — the charge voltage through the diode (~2.7 V) is below a CR2032's own 3.0 V, so no charge current flows: a plain CR2032 here is harmless. The hazard is feeding the module 5 V with a plain CR2032 fitted. If you must run it at 5 V, fit an LIR2032, or remove the diode or resistor.
  • 3.3 V data into a 5 V-powered clone bar usually works; if LED 1 flickers, add the series resistor or power the bar from 3V3 instead.
  • Wrong end (wiring DOUT) lights nothing — flip to DIN.
  • Active buzzer = one fixed pitch: you'll hear each pattern's rhythm but not its tones. Passive is the intended part.
  • Two wires in one XIAO pad is how a joint cracks. Chain instead.
  • Boots fine with the buzzer on D2. GPIO25 is sampled at power-up, but a passive buzzer straight to GND does not stop the XIAO C5 booting (bench-checked 2026-09-16 with the LED bar and buzzer wired as shown). If a different buzzer ever does, feed it through a small NPN transistor or a 100 Ω+ series resistor.
  • D3 (GPIO7) and an SD module: not bench-checked yet. GPIO7 may be sampled at boot, and an SD module usually pulls CS high. Unlike the buzzer-on-D2 note above, nobody has booted a C5 with an SD module wired yet. If the board stops booting with the card module fitted, unplug its CS wire and tell us.
