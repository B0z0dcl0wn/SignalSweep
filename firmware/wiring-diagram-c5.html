Wiring the C5 detector

All you need is the board. A bare XIAO ESP32-C5 with its dual-band U.FL antenna is a working detector: plug it into USB, connect the app, and it streams everything it hears. The buzzer, LED bar and DS3231 clock are optional add-ons, and the firmware checks at boot for what is fitted. The microSD slot is reserved: its wiring is drawn so a harness can include it, but the firmware does not use it yet.

Power chains

GND XIAO GND→bar GND═bar 2nd GND→RTC − The buzzer − piggybacks on the first bar GND pad, next to the wire in from the XIAO; the SD GND (reserved) piggybacks on the 2nd pad, next to the RTC wire. A bar pad is big enough for two wires, a XIAO pad is not. The RTC has one header, so it ends the chain. This assumes the common 4-pad bar: a 3-pad bar has one GND pad, which would need all four ground wires.
3V3 XIAO 3V3→RTC + One wire. The RTC is the only 3V3 load, and it cannot pass power on.
5V XIAO 5V→LED bar VCC→SD VCC For an SD module labelled VCC 5V (the common kind, with a regulator on board). A bare 3.3 V module has no clean 3V3 source in this harness, because the RTC can't pass power through; that gets settled when SD firmware support lands. Never feed 5 V to a bare 3.3 V card slot.

Assembly

  1. One wire per pad — chain the rest. The XIAO has one GND, one 3V3 and one 5V, each a tiny through-hole, and with every part fitted there are four grounds. Two wires shoved into one hole is how a joint cracks. So each XIAO pad gets exactly one wire, and ground hops from part to part (see Power chains). Any doubling up happens on a bar pad, never on the XIAO.
  2. LED bar. XIAO 5V → bar VCC, XIAO GND → the bar's first GND pad, XIAO D1 (GPIO0) → bar DIN. Wire the input end (the silkscreen arrows point into the LEDs). Optional 330–470 Ω resistor in series right at DIN.
  3. Buzzer. XIAO D2 (GPIO25) → buzzer +, buzzer − → that same first GND pad, next to the XIAO wire. A small passive buzzer (<30 mA) drives straight off the pin. 3-pin module? I/O → D2, GND → that same pad. Its VCC needs a supply: 3V3 is taken by the clock, so use the bar's VCC pad (5 V) only if the module is rated for 5 V.
  4. DS3231 clock. The small "DS3231 For Pi" module: one 5-pin female header, silkscreen + D C NC −. + ← XIAO 3V3, D ← D4 (GPIO23), C ← D5 (GPIO24), NC: no wire, − ← the bar's 2nd GND pad. With one header it passes nothing on, so it is the end of the chain. A ZS-042 works too: same pin names, and its second header can pass power through.
  5. microSD (reserved — skip it for now). If you build it into the harness anyway: CS ← D3 (GPIO7), SCK ← D8 (GPIO8), MISO → D9 (GPIO9), MOSI ← D10 (GPIO10), GND ← the bar's 2nd GND pad, next to the RTC wire. VCC: a VCC 5V module (the common kind, with a regulator) takes it from the bar's VCC pad. A bare 3.3 V module has no clean 3V3 source here, since the clock can't pass power on; that gets settled when SD firmware support lands. Pin order varies by module; match by label. The firmware does not drive it yet.
  6. Antenna. Swap the stock 2.4 GHz antenna for a dual-band U.FL one. The C5's whole point is 5 GHz; the one in the box throws it away.
  7. Verify. Flash from the web flasher, or python flash.py --board c5 --port COM5. On boot you get a short jingle and an LED blink (if fitted). The serial log says [RTC] DS3231 ok, clock set, [RTC] DS3231 lost power, waiting for a host, or [RTC] none fitted, waiting for a host. Connect the app: Settings › Device identity › Clock shows whether an RTC was found and that the phone synced it. Then trigger a match (an AirTag near it, or edit a signature): the bar flashes and the buzzer plays that category's pattern — ALPR / camera two long beeps, body cam long-short-short, drone a rising trill, tracker fast ticking. BOOT (GPIO28) is the onboard button: tap for a 2-minute advertising window, hold 5 s to factory-reset.

Watch for

Power budget ≈480 mA

  • 8× WS2812 at full white peaks near 480 mA. SignalSweep only flashes short colored bursts, so USB 5V is fine.
  • Add a 1000 µF cap across the bar's 5V↔GND to absorb inrush when all eight snap on.
  • The DS3231 and an SD module add a few mA idle, and roughly 100 mA peaks on SD writes. On a weak battery, budget for those.

Gotchas clones

  • I²C pull-ups: many "For Pi" DS3231 boards leave out the SDA/SCL pull-up resistors, because a Raspberry Pi has its own. The XIAO's internal pull-ups usually do on short wires. If the serial log says [RTC] none fitted with the module attached, add 4.7 kΩ from D4 to 3V3 and from D5 to 3V3.
  • "For Pi" module cell: check whether the coin cell is marked LIR2032 (rechargeable) or CR2032, and keep a replacement of the same kind.
  • ZS-042 + a CR2032: the board has a trickle-charge circuit (a resistor and diode near the header) meant for a rechargeable LIR2032. Fed from 3V3 — as the ZS-042 note above wires it — the charge voltage through the diode (~2.7 V) is below a CR2032's own 3.0 V, so no charge current flows: a plain CR2032 here is harmless. The hazard is feeding the module 5 V with a plain CR2032 fitted. If you must run it at 5 V, fit an LIR2032, or remove the diode or resistor.
  • 3.3 V data into a 5 V-powered clone bar usually works; if LED 1 flickers, add the series resistor or power the bar from 3V3 instead.
  • Wrong end (wiring DOUT) lights nothing — flip to DIN.
  • Active buzzer = one fixed pitch: you'll hear each pattern's rhythm but not its tones. Passive is the intended part.
  • Two wires in one XIAO pad is how a joint cracks. Chain instead.
  • Boots fine with the buzzer on D2. GPIO25 is sampled at power-up, but a passive buzzer straight to GND does not stop the XIAO C5 booting (bench-checked 2026-09-16 with the LED bar and buzzer wired as shown). If a different buzzer ever does, feed it through a small NPN transistor or a 100 Ω+ series resistor.
  • D3 (GPIO7) and an SD module: not bench-checked yet. GPIO7 may be sampled at boot, and an SD module usually pulls CS high. Unlike the buzzer-on-D2 note above, nobody has booted a C5 with an SD module wired yet. If the board stops booting with the card module fitted, unplug its CS wire and tell us.
