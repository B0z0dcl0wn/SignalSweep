SignalSweep

A headless RF detector that tells you what is near by the noise it makes. Plug it in and walk away — the phone is optional.

Flash it from this page

No Python, no PlatformIO, no drivers. Plug a XIAO ESP32-S3 in with a data-capable USB-C cable.

Open the app

Flashing needs Web Serial: desktop Chrome, Edge or Opera. The app itself also runs on Android Chrome over Bluetooth.

What it does

SignalSweep scans Bluetooth LE and Wi-Fi at the same time and matches what it hears against a signature list. When something matches it plays a different buzzer pattern per category — that is how a device with no screen tells you what it found, not just that it found something.

  • 📷 ALPR / camera two long beeps
  • 🎥 Body cam long · short · short
  • 🛸 Drone rising trill
  • 📍 Tracker fast ticking

Drone detection is a full ASTM F3411 Remote ID decode over BLE, Wi-Fi beacon and Wi-Fi NAN, so a drone is drawn at the real coordinate it broadcasts. Everything else is only ever roughly this far away, never a pin — that is all the radio actually knows, and a pin would be a lie.

What it deliberately does not do

This is a receiver. Offensive capability was removed on purpose, and should not grow back:

  • No jamming, no deauth, no packet injection.
  • No advertisement spoofing — the ble_spoof primitive was cut.
  • No arbitrary GATT writes. Ringing a tracker is one fixed Immediate Alert write, with a MAC as its only parameter.
  • No cloud, no telemetry, no analytics, no account.

It keeps no history. The app is a live scope, not a logbook: it shows what the device is matching right now and forgets it on disconnect. There is no passive location tracking. The only thing ever written to disk is a location pin you explicitly confirm, one device at a time, encrypted with AES-GCM behind a PIN — and detecting and beeping never ask for that PIN.

It can also stop advertising itself entirely. A counter-surveillance tool that announces SignalSweep to every scanner in range — including the hardware it is hunting — is doing the opposite of its job.

Get the hardware

One Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-S3, about $15. Add a U.FL antenna, a NeoPixel and a buzzer for the full build — see WIRING.md. It runs off any USB power: a battery pack, or a car's USB port.
